Snap the bottle, get the PT chart

Take a picture of the label on the refrigerant cylinder. The page reads the name and pulls up the PT chart. The photo stays on your phone.

Reading the label…

Get close so the name (like R-454B) fills the picture. Flat light, no glare. The first read downloads the reader, about 15 MB, so use Wi-Fi if you can.

Or tap your refrigerant

Red top = flammable (A2L). Same idea as the red band on an A2L cylinder.

PT chart

Below 0 psig is shown as inches of mercury vacuum (inHg). Every 5°F. The quick check above fills in between.

How I use it

  • Superheat: suction line temp minus the saturation temp at suction pressure. On a blend, use the vapor column.
  • Subcooling: saturation temp at liquid pressure minus liquid line temp. On a blend, use the liquid column.
  • Make sure your gauges are set to the same refrigerant. Wrong setting, wrong superheat.
Read the label, not the color. Cylinder colors are not a safe way to tell refrigerants apart anymore. Newer cylinders are mostly one gray color, with a red band or top for flammable ones. CHECK If the camera reads something you didn't expect, stop and read the label yourself.
Where these numbers come from

I worked these out with CoolProp 8.0, a free, open-source refrigerant property library built on published equations (2026-11-03). They are not copied from any maker's chart. Blend makeup used: R-454B 68.9% R-32 / 31.1% R-1234yf; R-452B 67/7/26 R-32/R-125/R-1234yf; R-407C 23/25/52 R-32/R-125/R-134a; R-404A 44/52/4 R-125/R-143a/R-134a; R-410A 50/50 R-32/R-125. Small differences (about 1 to 2 psi) from a maker's chart are normal. CHECK Spot-check against the refrigerant maker's chart before you trust it for a charge.
