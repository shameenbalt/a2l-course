A2L Refrigerants Explained for Techs

By Shameen · HVAC service tech, 20 years in the field · Updated Oct 26, 2026

Quick answer

A2L refrigerants like R-454B and R-32 are the new standard in US air conditioning and heat pumps. "A" means lower toxicity. "2L" means lower flammability: they can burn, but they are hard to light and the flame is slow. They replaced R-410A because EPA limits new AC and heat pump systems to refrigerants under 700 GWP. For you on the job, that means leak sensors in the equipment, red-band cylinders with left-hand threads, A2L-rated tools, and no brazing until the system is recovered and purged.

I've been in HVAC about 20 years. I worked through the R-22 to R-410A change, and this one is bigger, because for the first time the refrigerant in a normal split system or VRF can burn. It's not scary once you know the rules. Here's what I tell newer techs.

What does A2L mean?

It's a safety class from ASHRAE Standard 34, the standard that gives every refrigerant its R-number.

  • A or B is toxicity. A is lower toxicity, B is higher.
  • 1, 2L, 2, 3 is flammability. 1 means no flame spread (R-410A, R-22). 3 is highly flammable (R-290 propane).
  • 2L sits at the bottom of class 2. The flame has to burn at 10 cm/s or slower (about 4 inches a second). You need a real ignition source and the right mix in the air for it to light.

So R-410A is A1. R-454B and R-32 are A2L.

Why the change happened

The AIM Act of 2020 lets EPA phase down HFC production and import by 85% by 2036. Under EPA's Technology Transitions rule, new residential and light commercial AC and heat pump systems made or imported from January 1, 2025 have to use a refrigerant under 700 GWP. VRF got a later date.

In May 2026 EPA removed the January 1, 2026 install deadline for R-410A systems made or imported before 2025, so leftover R-410A stock can still be installed until it's gone. Servicing existing R-410A systems was never banned. CHECK EPA keeps adjusting this rule. Look up the current dates for your equipment type before you tell a customer.

The leak sensor in the unit (RDS)

This is the part that surprises people on their first A2L job. Equipment is listed to UL 60335-2-40, and most A2L equipment needs a built-in refrigerant detection system (RDS). UL says it's generally expected above about 4 lb of charge, which covers almost every split system and VRF.

  • The sensor sits low, near the bottom of the indoor coil. A2L vapor is heavier than air.
  • At 25% of the lower flammability limit, it has to call for mitigation within 15 seconds and hold it for at least 5 minutes.
  • Typical mitigation: run the indoor blower, open zone dampers, shut off the compressor, kill electric heat, and close shutoff valves on multi-coil systems.

Field tip: if a brand-new A2L system has the blower running nonstop with no call, or locks out the compressor, check the RDS before you chase anything else. Read the install manual for that sensor's fault codes and wiring.

Tools and cylinders

  • Cylinders are gray with the R-number printed on them. A2L cylinders have a red band around the top.
  • Most A2L cylinders use a left-hand thread valve (CGA 164). Buy the adapter before you need it.
  • Recovery machine and vacuum pump: use ones rated for A2L. They're built so switches and motors don't spark.
  • Recovery cylinders must be rated for flammable refrigerant.
  • Leak detector: use one that lists R-454B or R-32. Bubbles still work to pinpoint.
  • R-32 and R-454B ship as DOT Division 2.1 flammable gas. Know your truck's limits. CHECK

Safe service, step by step

  1. Check the work area with your leak detector first. Make sure there's airflow.
  2. Keep ignition sources away: torches, grinders, cigarettes, non-rated tools.
  3. Charge R-454B as liquid and weigh it in to the nameplate. Then use the maker's charging chart.
  4. Never mix refrigerants. R-454B is not a drop-in for R-410A.
  5. Before brazing: recover, evacuate, purge with dry nitrogen, and braze with nitrogen flowing.
  6. Recover, never vent. Low GWP doesn't make venting legal.
  7. If anything burns, ventilate and stay out of the smoke. Burning refrigerant can make hydrogen fluoride.

Charge size is also limited by the size of the room the system serves (the "minimum room area" tables in the install manual). That matters most on ductless and VRF jobs with small rooms.

Do you need a new certification?

Your EPA Section 608 card still covers it. EPA doesn't issue a separate A2L card. But take an A2L safety course anyway. Many manufacturers and employers require one, and the hands-on parts (cylinders, sensors, recovery) are worth it. CHECK

Common questions

Is R-454B a drop-in for R-410A?

No. Don't top off or convert an R-410A system with R-454B. A2L equipment is built and listed for it, with sensors, labels and charge limits.

Are A2L refrigerants dangerous?

They're mildly flammable and low toxicity. With the right tools and steps they're safe to work on. The risk is a big leak in a small closed room plus a spark or flame, which is exactly what the sensors and room-size limits are there to prevent.

Can I still buy and install R-410A equipment?

Equipment made or imported before 2025 can still be installed under EPA's May 2026 change. New production for residential and light commercial AC has moved to A2L. Check EPA for the current rule.

Which standard covers what?

ASHRAE 34 names and classes the refrigerant. ASHRAE 15 (and 15.2 for homes) covers safety in the building. UL 60335-2-40 covers the equipment itself. Your local mechanical code decides which editions apply.
