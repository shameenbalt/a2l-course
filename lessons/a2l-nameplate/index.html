Read the A2L Nameplate

By Shameen · HVAC service tech · Oct 29, 2026 · About 8 minutes

Warm-up: 3 quick guesses

Guess first. Nothing here is scored.

On a rooftop startup this week, the first thing I noticed on the nameplate wasn't the volts or the amps. It was "MINIMUM ROOM AREA" in the biggest letters on the plate, and a flame symbol in the corner. That's the new normal. Here's how to read one.

1Tap the plate

This is a sample I drew, with made-up numbers. It's laid out the way a real A2L rooftop unit plate is. Tap a number to see what that line means.

2The 3 lines I check first

  1. Refrigerant. Match your gauges, your cylinder and your recovery tank to it. Never top off with something else.
  2. Charge per circuit. Each circuit is its own system. Know the number before you recover or charge.
  3. Minimum room area. If the unit sits indoors or gets stored in a space smaller than that, stop and ask.

Divide ounces by 16, not 10. And on a 2-circuit unit, don't stop at circuit 1.

3Bonus: the disconnect

⚡ Looks like a breaker. Isn't one. Many new rooftop units come with a molded case switch as the disconnect. Read its label: it says it provides no overcurrent protection. It won't trip on an overload. The breaker or fuses feeding the unit do that job, and they can't be bigger than the nameplate's max overcurrent protection.
Explain it back

Say these out loud like you're showing a helper the plate.

  1. What does UL 60335-2-40 on the plate tell you?
  2. A 2-circuit unit says 288 oz per circuit. What's the total in pounds?
  3. What is minimum room area, and why is it on the plate?
  4. Why isn't a molded case switch the unit's overload protection?
