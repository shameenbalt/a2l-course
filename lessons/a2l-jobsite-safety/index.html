A2L guide · Lesson 1

A2L Jobsite Safety

By Shameen · HVAC service tech · Oct 27, 2026 · About 10 minutes

Warm-up: 3 quick guesses

Guess before you read. Getting one wrong is fine. It makes the answer stick when you get to it below. Nothing here is scored.

This lesson covers what changes on a normal service call once the refrigerant can burn: the tools you bring, how you haul the cylinder, what you look for when you walk in, and the label you fill out when you leave. I wrote it while studying for my own A2L certification.

1Know what you're carrying: A1, A2L, A3

Three classes come up all day now. Know them cold.

  • A1 doesn't spread a flame. R-410A, R-22.
  • A2L can burn, but it's hard to light and the flame is slow and weak. R-454B, R-32.
  • A3 is highly flammable. R-290 (propane), R-600a (isobutane). It lights easy and burns hot and fast, so A3 systems hold a very small charge.

Here's how I keep it straight: an A2L needs a lot to go wrong at once. An A3 doesn't. Don't let "mildly flammable" make you careless, but don't treat an A2L like a propane tank either.

2Tools and cylinders

  • Spark-proof where it counts. Your recovery machine and vacuum pump should be rated for A2L. A standard motor or switch can throw a spark right where the vapor is. Gauges and hoses are fine. Your leak detector has to be made to sense A2L.
  • Red is the warning. A2L cylinders have a red band or red top. CHECK
  • Left-hand threads. A2L cylinders use a left-hand thread valve, so you can't screw an A1 adapter on by mistake. Righty-tighty doesn't apply here. CHECK
  • Never mix or top off. R-454B is not a drop-in for R-410A. Recover into a cylinder marked for that refrigerant only.

3Supply house to the job

Most cylinder problems start before you get to the job.

  • At the counter: check the label, the red band, and that the valve is shut and capped. Don't load a cylinder that's dented, rusted or hissing.
  • In the truck: stand it upright and strap it down. Keep it out of the cab, in a space that gets air, and away from heat and the sun.
  • DOT: A2L refrigerant ships as a Division 2.1 flammable gas. Carrying it as a tech usually falls under the "materials of trade" rules in 49 CFR, which set limits on how much you carry and how it's secured. CHECK
  • Leak alarm in the truck? I couldn't find a DOT rule that requires one. A portable A2L detector in the truck is still smart. CHECK
If you hear hissing while you drive: pull over somewhere safe, shut off the engine, get out, and open the doors so it airs out. Don't smoke and don't flip switches. Wait until the leak stops and the air clears before you touch it.

4Walk in and look for fire

Before I open a system, I do a quick lap and ask two questions. What could light it? And where would the gas go?

  • Things that light it: pilot lights, energized heat strips, a coworker smoking, a tool or light switch that sparks, a running motor that isn't rated for A2L.
  • Where it goes: A2L vapor is heavier than air. It sinks and pools low, in a closed room, a pit or a closet, right where water heater pilots sit.
  • What I do: kill or isolate ignition sources, get air moving, check the area with my detector, and keep it checked while I work.

Picture this: a closet with a gas water heater and an air handler. The coil leaks overnight with the door shut. The pilot is 6 inches off the floor. That's exactly where the gas collects. Shut the gas and open the door before you even pull gauges.

5Fill out the label before you leave

On A2L installs, field information goes on the unit. Honestly, it's good practice on every system. The next tech shouldn't have to guess.

A2L SYSTEM LABEL (SAMPLE) CompanySmith Heating & Air Evacuation450 microns Leak test400 psig dry nitrogen Test date10/27/2026 RefrigerantR-454B Total charge9 lb 4 oz

Total charge means the factory charge plus what you added for the line set. Leave off anything that isn't about the system, like the customer's phone number.
